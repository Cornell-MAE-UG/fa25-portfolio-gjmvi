## Overview

This project was a core part of MAE 2250 and focuses on the complete design process, from initial idea to full CAD and prototype, and finally to the finished project and executive report. The project focused on the removal of the invasive spotted lantern flies (SLFs), and their removal from vineyards. Groups of students created novel ideas and then worked them through the entire process.

## Client Pitch (O3)
<a id="client-pitch"></a>

### Problem Statement

The spotted lantern fly (SLF) is an invasive species in North America. Economic losses to the grape industry in the Finger Lakes alone are estimated to reach $1.5 million in the first year of infestation and double annually. Infested grapevines use 38% less water due to reduced sap flow, leading to lower quality products. SLFs also remain on grapevines during harvesting, contaminating grapes and further decreasing yields. Currently, SLF removal is only performed during harvesting and processing. 

---

### Impact

Passive SLF population control during the growing season will reduce overall vine damage and limit contamination during harvesting. 

---

### Proposed Direction

**Concept: “The Lure”**

**What it is:**  
A device hung from a trellis that attracts and contains SLFs.

**Minimum Viable Product:**  
A plastic bottle filled with wintergreen oil, with a rubber valve acting as a one-way entrance.

**Potential Long-Term Product:**  
A dual-attraction system combining wintergreen oil and a 60 Hz speaker. A helical internal wall guides SLFs into a replaceable containment chamber. 

---

### Key Risks / Unknowns

- **Cost:** Deploying a lure at each grapevine could be expensive — scalability is uncertain.  
- **Efficacy:** Measuring attraction effectiveness is difficult in a controlled classroom environment.  
- **Maintenance:** Balancing manual labor requirements with effectiveness is a challenge. 

---

### Questions for the Client

1. What financial investment is available for full-scale deployment?  
2. How much labor is available for maintaining the system?  
3. What level of structural attachment is required to ensure durability through growing and harvesting seasons?
